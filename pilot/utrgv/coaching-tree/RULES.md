# EDGE3 Coaching Tree Rules

Added October 22, 2026 (Kenyon). These govern how coach baselines, position archetypes and comps are built for any program, starting with the UTRGV pilot. They belong next to the modeling principles in the edge3-model CLAUDE.md; a copy lives here with the UTRGV tree.

## Rule 1. Coach history, not program history

The baseline for a position is the history of the coach who runs that room, not the history of the program. A program's pooled history is context only. Read a recruit against what this coach has produced at every stop of his career.

- For a head coach, go back through every assistant stop (coordinator, position coach, GA where the role is documented) and his own playing career for context. A first-time head coach has no head-coaching history to read, so the assistant stops are the record. Coach Bush: Texas State, UTSA, Houston, the Bills fellowship, Seguin and Canyon.
- For each position coach, do the same for every stop where he ran a position group or a side of the ball.
- Source order for staff history: the coach's official school bio, prior-school bios, team media guides (the only reliable source for OC, DC and position-coach assignments by year), conference releases, then press. CFBD covers FBS head coaches only; assistant and FCS histories need their own source.
- When the coach names his own benchmark players (the Positional Baseline Workbook), those names outrank the researched history. Where the two disagree, flag it for the coach rather than averaging.

## Rule 2. Map every position coach to the players he coached, ranked

Every position coach is mapped to the players he coached at each stop, in two lists, TOP and LOW, and both lists are sorted highest to lowest by the same key:

1. **NFL outcome first.** Drafted, on an NFL roster, or NFL honors rank above undrafted free agents, camp invites, practice squad and other pro leagues, which rank above no pro outcome.
2. **Production second.** All-American or conference/national player of the year, then first-team all-conference, then second team or newcomer/freshman/rookie of the year, then third team, honorable mention, all-region or academic honors, then school records or team-leading production, then stats only.
3. **PFF grades third.** Best PFF season grade during the coach's years at that stop, matched by player, team and position group. PFF covers FBS 2014 to 2025 in the repo; FCS pulls exist from 2025 only. A blank PFF cell at an FCS, D2, JUCO, high school or pre-2014 stop is expected, not a gap in the player.

A player is LOW only when a source documents the outcome (benched, transferred out after a down year, dismissed, an "expected breakout" that did not produce). Never label a player low on judgment alone. Never train on the LOW set; it is a list of documented failure modes to check a recruit against.

## Rule 3. Every row carries its source

Every timeline row and every player row carries the URL it came from. Anything that could not be confirmed is marked unverified or not found, never guessed. Disagreements between sources are reported, not silently resolved.

## Where this applies

- Position archetypes and the System view in the pilot.
- Comp selection: the comp pool starts with rooms the coach knows personally (the UTRGV comps script already prioritizes Texas State, UTSA and Houston for Coach Bush), then regional, then the full cohort.
- Recruit reads such as the Chase Ames UTRGV Risk/Fit wallet, which anchored him to the Marburger arc in Coach Bush's own passing system rather than to UTRGV's one-season program history.
