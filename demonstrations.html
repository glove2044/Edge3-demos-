Executive Case Studies

How the operating model performs against real decisions.

Each demonstration follows the same discipline: strategic objective, critical decision, capability gap, configured solution, business value, transferable learning. No stories. No hype. The operating model, applied.

Demonstration 01 — Football Operations

Revenue-Share Allocation as Portfolio Strategy

A football front office allocating a fixed revenue-share pool across returning starters, incoming transfers, and high school signees.

Strategic Objective

Maximize on-field return on a fixed revenue-share pool while keeping enough allocation flexibility to act in both transfer portal windows.

Critical Decision

How much to commit, to which athletes, at which points in the calendar — knowing every early commitment reduces optionality later, and every delay risks losing a target.

Capability Gap

Allocation decisions were made athlete-by-athlete under deadline pressure, with no portfolio-level view of exposure by position, class, or retention risk — and no consistent standard connecting compensation to projected contribution.

Configured Solution

EDGE3 configured a portfolio view of the entire revenue-share pool: projected contribution and retention risk per athlete, exposure by position group, and scenario models showing how each proposed commitment changed remaining flexibility across the roster calendar.

Business Value

Leadership negotiates from a defensible standard rather than market panic. Capital concentrates on athletes who fit how the program wins, and the department enters each portal window with deliberate reserve capacity instead of discovering its constraints mid-negotiation.

Transferable Learning

Any organization allocating scarce capital across a talent portfolio — professional franchises, private equity operating companies — faces the same structure: individual decisions made without portfolio context destroy value silently.

Demonstration 02 — Basketball Operations

Retention Before the Portal Opens

A basketball program protecting two seasons of development investment as the transfer portal window approaches.

Strategic Objective

Retain the developed athletes the program has already invested in — the cheapest and highest-certainty roster capital available to any department.

Critical Decision

Which athletes need leadership attention now, what form that attention should take, and where retention capital is best deployed — weeks before the portal makes the question public.

Capability Gap

The staff's picture of athlete engagement lived in fragments — position coach impressions, academic reports, informal conversations. By the time disengagement was visible to everyone, the athlete was already in the portal and the leverage was gone.

Configured Solution

EDGE3 consolidated engagement signals across coaching interactions, development milestones, and communication patterns into a single retention view — surfacing which athletes required attention, why, and what the cost of losing each would be against their replacement in the market.

Business Value

Retention becomes proactive rather than reactive. The program addresses risk while it still has standing to do so, protects its revenue-share investment in developed athletes, and avoids paying market premiums to replace contributors it already had.

Transferable Learning

Attrition is never the first signal — it is the last. Organizations that consolidate engagement signals retain their best people at a fraction of replacement cost. The mechanics transfer directly to enterprise workforce retention.

Demonstration 03 — Athletic Department Leadership

Institutional Knowledge Through a Coaching Transition

An athletic department preserving evaluation standards, recruiting relationships, and decision rationale across a head coaching change.

Strategic Objective

Ensure the department's accumulated intelligence — not just its rosters — survives leadership transitions, so each new staff builds on the last rather than starting over.

Critical Decision

What the incoming staff needs to know on day one: which evaluations were trusted and why, which relationships carry history, which commitments carry obligations, and what the departing staff learned that never made it into a file.

Capability Gap

Institutional knowledge lived in the departing staff's phones, inboxes, and memories. Every transition historically reset recruiting relationships, evaluation standards, and athlete context to zero — a cost the department paid in full with every hire.

Configured Solution

Because EDGE3 operates inside daily decisions, the record already existed: evaluation rationale, relationship history, communication context, and outcome data, captured continuously as work product rather than assembled retrospectively. The transition became a transfer of intelligence, not a loss of it.

Business Value

The department de-risks its most expensive recurring event. New leadership reaches full decision quality in weeks instead of seasons, current athletes experience continuity instead of uncertainty, and the institution — not any individual — owns its intelligence.

Transferable Learning

Knowledge captured as a byproduct of execution survives; knowledge captured through exit interviews does not. This is the core argument for decision infrastructure in any organization where expertise concentrates in people who eventually leave.
